Remorse Chess Strategy

Plan for a bad roll before you take a piece. These three positions show how to set up a bishop rescue, spot a two-move win, and decide when to decline a bloodlust bonus.

Choose who makes the first capture

Half of all first captures leave the piece devastated or guilt-ridden. If your queen and a pawn can both take the same piece and the replies are roughly equal, consider the pawn. A bad roll then affects a less valuable piece, and your queen can still recapture on another square.

Check the piece’s status before you calculate a trade. An unaffected or absolved piece that already rolled can capture again with no die roll. A guilt-ridden piece can block a line or move out of trouble, but it cannot recapture. If it appears to guard an important square, count the other defenders.

Set up a bishop rescue before capturing

Play Nc2×d4. If the knight rolls a 1, it freezes. After Black’s reply, select the bishop on c3, select the marked knight on d4, and choose Absolve. The bishop stays on c3 and your turn ends. The knight can move and capture normally on its next turn.

The diagonal adjacency saves a whole move. If the roll is 2 or 3, there’s no deadline. You can develop another piece and heal the knight when you need its captures back. If a devastated piece is about to disappear, also check whether its square plugs a line to your king.

Spot a two-move king capture

Play Re7×e4+, then use the same rook’s bonus move for Re4×e1. Black wins before White gets to respond. The rook already rolled its 6, so the first capture in this sequence earns a bonus without a new roll.

To defend this threat, check the landing square of the first capture. If it opens a legal path to your king, deal with it on the turn before the capture. Moving the king off the line or removing the target can break the combination.

Count to three before spending a bonus move

After Nc2×d4, the knight gains bloodlust immediately. It can jump to e6 and take its own pawn on the bonus move. The quiet jump to b5 is illegal because b5 is empty.

Choose End turn to keep your pawn unless taking it serves a concrete purpose. Before committing to the third capture, look one turn ahead. If the landing square has few future capture targets, you may prefer to move the knight to a better square while it still has that option.
